"The basic structure doctrine has protected the Indian Constitution from losing its essence, but it has also led to judicial overreach." Critically analyze this statement.
GS 2 Indian Polity 15 Marks

Tips

  • Kesavananda Bharati case (1973) established that Parliament cannot alter the 'Basic Structure' under Article 368.
  • Saved democracy during the 1975 Emergency, protected judicial review (Minerva Mills case), and prevented absolute majoritarianism.
  • 'Basic Structure' is nowhere defined in the Constitution. Courts use it subjectively (e.g., striking down the NJAC Act), sometimes acting as a "super-legislature."
  • The doctrine is a necessary safety valve, but the judiciary must exercise self-restraint to respect the Separation of Powers.
